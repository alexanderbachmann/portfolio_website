Every company I have worked with — from central banks to tech giants — has more data than it knows what to do with. Dashboards multiply, pipelines hum along, and yet the same question keeps surfacing in leadership meetings: *"Why can't we get a straight answer out of our data?"*

The answer is rarely technical. It is almost always a question of ownership.

## Data as a product, not a byproduct

Most organizations treat data as exhaust — something that accumulates as a side effect of running the business. Reports get built on request, pipelines get patched when they break, and nobody is accountable for whether any of it actually drives a decision.

A data *product* is different. It has:

- **An owner** who is accountable for its adoption, not just its uptime
- **Users** whose problems it demonstrably solves
- **A roadmap** that evolves with the business, not with the backlog
- **A definition of done** that includes trust — documented, tested, and understood

When a dataset has an owner, "is this number right?" has an answer and a name attached to it. When it doesn't, every consumer builds their own version of the truth, and the organization pays for the same answer five times.

## What ownership looks like in practice

Ownership is not a job title — it is a set of behaviors:

1. **Saying no.** A product owner curates. Not every request deserves a dashboard; most deserve a conversation about the decision behind the request.
2. **Measuring adoption, not delivery.** Shipping a data model is the start. If nobody changes a decision because of it, it failed — no matter how elegant the pipeline.
3. **Aligning teams before aligning schemas.** The hardest joins are organizational. Two teams with different definitions of "active customer" is a product problem wearing a technical costume.

## The payoff

Teams that adopt real ownership over their data products stop arguing about numbers and start arguing about decisions — which is exactly where the argument should be.

That is the difference between data and noise.
